Here's the problem, in one story

Two friends open a lemonade stand. Day one, they agree: profits split right down the middle, 50/50.

Then real life happens. One friend shows up every single day — squeezing lemons, making the sign, talking to customers. The other shows up twice, then stops coming.

Nobody wants to be the one who says "hey, maybe it shouldn't be 50/50 anymore." That conversation is awkward, and it can end a friendship. So nobody says it. The split just stays wrong — for the whole life of the stand.

That's exactly what happens to companies, DAOs, and teams of AI agents building something together. Ownership gets frozen on day one, before anyone knows who's actually going to do the work.

Here's the fix

The Living Cap Table never lets that gap open up in the first place. Instead of agreeing on a split once and hoping it stays fair, it keeps checking — automatically, honestly, and without anyone having to bring it up.

Ownership isn't negotiated. It's read, straight off of what people actually did.

How it actually works

1

Write the rule

Whoever starts the team writes one simple rule in plain English, like: "give credit for real, useful work — not just for showing up."

2

Everyone signs up

Each person (or AI agent) joins with their real, public GitHub username. No fake profiles, no self-reported hours.

3

A panel of AIs checks the real work

Not one AI — several, running different models, so no single opinion decides anything. They look at everyone's actual public activity and score it against the rule.

4

The split updates itself

No meeting. No vote. No one has to ask. The ownership numbers just move to match what really happened.

Why you can trust the number

This runs on GenLayer, a network where several independent AI validators all have to look at the same evidence and agree before anything is written down. If they don't agree, nothing changes.

So it's never "one AI's opinion." It's a panel, checking real public evidence, that has to reach consensus — the same way you'd trust a decision more if five honest judges agreed on it than if one person just declared it.
